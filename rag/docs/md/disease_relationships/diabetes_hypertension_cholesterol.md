# Diabetes, Hypertension and Cholesterol: Relationship Knowledge

## Why these conditions are linked

Diabetes, hypertension and dyslipidemia frequently occur together and can collectively increase cardiovascular and kidney risk.

They share several risk factors:

- Physical inactivity
- Excess body weight
- Unhealthy dietary patterns
- Smoking
- Metabolic dysfunction
- Family history and genetics
- Increasing age

## Diabetes and hypertension

Diabetes and hypertension together increase cardiovascular and kidney risk. Blood pressure management is therefore an important component of diabetes care.

## Diabetes and cholesterol

Diabetes is associated with increased cardiovascular risk. Lipid assessment is part of cardiovascular risk management in people with diabetes.

## Hypertension and cholesterol

High blood pressure and elevated LDL can both contribute to cardiovascular disease. Their combined presence can increase overall risk.

## Metabolic syndrome concept

A cluster of metabolic abnormalities can include:

- Increased waist circumference
- Elevated blood pressure
- Elevated blood glucose
- Elevated triglycerides
- Low HDL cholesterol

Metabolic syndrome is a risk pattern rather than a single disease.

## Human activity

Physical activity is a cross-condition intervention:

- Supports insulin sensitivity
- Supports blood-pressure control
- Supports cardiovascular fitness
- Can improve lipid profiles
- Supports weight management
- Reduces sedentary behavior

## RAG reasoning pattern

When a user has several risk factors, the system should reason across them rather than treating each disease independently.

Example:
`Low activity + obesity + hypertension + diabetes -> increased cardiometabolic risk`

The system must describe this as increased risk, not predict that the patient will develop a specific complication.

## Monitoring relationship

Potential monitoring variables:

- Blood glucose / HbA1c
- Blood pressure
- LDL, HDL and triglycerides
- Weight and waist-related measures when clinically appropriate
- Physical activity
- Sedentary time
- Sleep
- Heart rate

No single wearable or home measurement can provide a complete assessment of cardiometabolic health.
