# Human Activity to Disease Relationship Knowledge

## Purpose

This document provides relationship statements that can be retrieved by a RAG system when explaining how daily activity may relate to diabetes, hypertension and cholesterol.

## Diabetes relationships

### Physical activity and glucose

Regular physical activity increases muscle glucose utilization and improves insulin sensitivity. Over time, this can support glycemic management.

### Sedentary time and diabetes

Long periods of inactivity are associated with less favorable metabolic health. Breaking up prolonged sitting is recommended as part of diabetes activity guidance.

### Exercise and weight

Regular activity contributes to energy expenditure and can support weight management when combined with an appropriate eating pattern.

## Hypertension relationships

### Aerobic activity and blood pressure

Regular aerobic activity can help lower blood pressure and improve cardiovascular fitness.

### Sodium and blood pressure

Higher sodium intake can increase blood pressure. Reducing sodium is a population-level strategy for reducing cardiovascular risk.

### Sedentary behavior

Physical inactivity is a modifiable hypertension risk factor.

## Cholesterol relationships

### Physical activity and LDL

Regular activity can help improve lipid levels, including LDL cholesterol.

### Physical activity and HDL

Regular activity can support higher HDL levels, although HDL should not be interpreted as a standalone treatment target.

### Diet and LDL

Reducing excessive saturated and trans fats and emphasizing a heart-healthy dietary pattern can support LDL management.

## Cross-disease relationships

`Regular physical activity`
→ improved insulin sensitivity
→ improved cardiovascular fitness
→ supports blood pressure management
→ can improve lipid profile
→ supports weight management

`Physical inactivity`
→ reduced energy expenditure
→ increased cardiometabolic risk
→ can contribute to diabetes and hypertension risk

`High sodium intake`
→ increased blood pressure risk
→ increased cardiovascular risk

`High saturated-fat intake`
→ may increase LDL cholesterol
→ contributes to cardiovascular risk when LDL remains elevated

## Important limitation

These are population-level physiological and epidemiological relationships. They do not establish that one observed behavior caused a specific patient's measurement or complication.
