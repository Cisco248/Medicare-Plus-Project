# Medical Safety and Red Flags

## General rule

A RAG system should never reassure a user that a potentially serious symptom is harmless based only on general knowledge.

## Possible cardiovascular/stroke emergencies

Urgent/emergency evaluation may be required for:

- Chest pressure or severe chest pain
- Severe difficulty breathing
- Fainting or loss of consciousness
- New facial drooping
- New weakness or numbness, especially on one side
- New difficulty speaking or understanding speech
- Sudden severe neurological symptoms
- Severe or rapidly worsening illness

## Diabetes-related urgent concerns

Seek urgent medical evaluation for severe symptoms such as:

- Loss of consciousness
- Severe confusion
- Repeated vomiting
- Difficulty breathing
- Severe dehydration
- Severe weakness
- Concerning symptoms associated with markedly abnormal glucose

People using insulin or medicines capable of causing hypoglycemia should have an individualized hypoglycemia plan from their healthcare professional.

## Hypertension-related urgent concerns

A markedly elevated blood pressure measurement with severe symptoms such as chest pain, severe shortness of breath, neurological deficits, confusion or fainting warrants urgent assessment.

A single high reading without severe symptoms should not automatically be labeled an emergency; repeat measurement using appropriate technique and seek clinical advice when readings remain high.

## Cholesterol

High cholesterol usually does not cause acute symptoms. It is primarily a long-term cardiovascular risk factor. Do not attribute acute chest pain or neurological symptoms to "cholesterol" and stop there; urgent causes must be considered.

## RAG safety response pattern

When a red flag is detected:

1. State that the symptom may require urgent assessment.
2. Avoid diagnosis.
3. Recommend contacting local emergency/urgent medical services as appropriate.
4. Do not bury the warning under lifestyle advice.
