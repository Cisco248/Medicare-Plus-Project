# Sleep and Chronic Disease

## Definition and purpose

Sleep is a recurring biological state essential to physical and mental health. Duration, quality, timing, and regularity can be associated with metabolic and cardiovascular health, but sleep data alone cannot diagnose chronic disease.

## Key facts

Adults generally need seven or more hours of sleep, although individual need varies. Persistent insufficient or disrupted sleep is associated with diabetes, hypertension, cardiovascular risk, mood, and impaired daytime function. Association does not establish that sleep caused a condition. Work schedules, caregiving, stress, environment, pain, illness, medicines, alcohol, and sleep disorders all affect sleep.

## Wearable data

Consumer devices estimate sleep from movement, heart rate, and related signals. They may misclassify quiet wakefulness, naps, or device non-wear and do not replace clinical sleep studies. Sleep-stage percentages and proprietary scores should be treated as estimates.

## Safe interpretation

Analyze repeated, sufficiently complete records against a stated personal baseline. One short night is not insomnia; long recorded duration does not prove good sleep. Missing data are unknown. Do not diagnose sleep apnea, insomnia, diabetes, hypertension, or high cholesterol from duration, sleep stages, steps, or heart rate. Do not recommend changing medicine.

## Routine clinical advice

Seek routine advice for persistent sleep difficulty, excessive daytime sleepiness, loud snoring or witnessed breathing pauses, safety concerns from sleepiness, or sleep problems affecting chronic-condition management. Sudden severe symptoms should be escalated based on those symptoms, not a sleep score.
