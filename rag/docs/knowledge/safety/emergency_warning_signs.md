# Emergency Warning Signs

## Purpose and priority

This document has the highest retrieval priority when severe or rapidly worsening symptoms are described. It supports escalation, not diagnosis. Do not wait for another day of wearable data when emergency warning signs are present.

## Contact local emergency services

Contact local emergency services for:

- severe or persistent chest pain, pressure, or tightness;
- major difficulty breathing;
- collapse, loss of consciousness, or inability to wake normally;
- sudden facial weakness, arm weakness, speech difficulty, severe confusion, or sudden major vision change;
- seizure;
- severe allergic reaction with breathing difficulty or swelling;
- uncontrolled heavy bleeding; or
- any rapidly worsening severe symptom or immediate threat to life.

Use the local emergency service for the person’s location; do not assume a country-specific number. If it is safe, follow instructions from emergency professionals and any established emergency care plan.

## Safe RAG behavior

- Lead with concise emergency action and location-appropriate language.
- Do not diagnose the cause or minimize symptoms because steps, heart rate, sleep, or calories appear normal.
- Do not delay for repeated measurements or more complete data.
- Do not recommend starting, stopping, taking extra, or changing medicine.
- Do not turn every mild symptom into an emergency. Severity, sudden onset, progression, and associated warning signs matter.

## Non-emergency concerns

Persistent or recurring symptoms without emergency features should receive routine or prompt clinical advice according to severity and context. Wearable alerts can be useful prompts to check wellbeing or validated measurements, but an alert alone is not a diagnosis.
