# Heart Disease Overview

Also called: cardiac disease, heart condition, cardiovascular disease, coronary heart disease.

## Question: What is heart disease?

### Answer

Heart disease is a group of conditions that affect the heart’s blood vessels, muscle, rhythm, or valves. Coronary artery disease (narrowing of arteries that supply the heart) is a common form and can lead to angina or heart attack. Other forms include heart failure, arrhythmias, valve disease, and cardiomyopathy.

### Important safety information

This is education, not a diagnosis. Severe chest pain, major breathing difficulty, collapse, or sudden stroke signs need local emergency services immediately. A MediCare Plus “High heart-disease risk” label is not a heart-attack diagnosis.

## Coronary artery disease and heart attack

Plaque can narrow coronary arteries. A heart attack occurs when blood flow to heart muscle is blocked. NHS and AHA list chest pressure, breathlessness, and radiating pain as warning symptoms. Women, older adults, and people with diabetes may notice less classic symptoms.

## Heart failure, arrhythmias, valves, cardiomyopathy

Heart failure means the heart cannot pump as well as the body needs. Arrhythmias are rhythm problems. Valve disease affects one-way heart valves. Cardiomyopathy is disease of the heart muscle. These need clinical tests; the e-doc survey cannot separate them.

## Risk factors

Age, male sex on average, smoking, diabetes, high blood pressure, unfavorable cholesterol, obesity, inactivity, family history, previous stroke, and poor self-rated health. Several of these are heart-disease model features.

## Symptoms

Chest discomfort, breathlessness, ankle swelling, palpitations, reduced exercise tolerance, or collapse. Absence of symptoms does not prove healthy arteries.

## Diagnosis concepts

Clinicians may use history, ECG, blood tests including troponin in emergencies, echocardiogram, stress testing, or angiography. The app model uses survey features and BMI only.

## Management concepts

Lifestyle, risk-factor control, and prescribed medicines (antiplatelets, statins, blood-pressure drugs, and others when indicated) are educational pillars. Rehabilitation programs are clinical services. Do not start or stop cardiac medicines from chat.
