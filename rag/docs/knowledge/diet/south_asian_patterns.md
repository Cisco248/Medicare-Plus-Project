# South Asian and Sri Lankan Eating Patterns

Diabetes, hypertension, and heart-disease education can use local foods without banning culture.

## Staples

Rice, red rice, string hoppers, hoppers, roti, and bread are carbohydrate foods. Portions, cooking fat, and what they are eaten with matter more than a universal ban.

## Protein and pulses

Dhal, other lentils, beans, fish, eggs, chicken, and yoghurt can support a mixed meal.

## Vegetables and sambols

Leafy greens, mixed vegetable curries, and sambols add flavor. Salt, coconut, and oil amounts vary.

## Drinks and sweets

Sweet tea, fizzy drinks, and desserts add free sugar. Water and unsweetened tea are generally better everyday choices.

## Salt and frying

Packaged snacks, processed fish or meats, and repeated deep frying can raise sodium and unhealthy fat. WHO salt guidance is less than 5 g salt per day for adults unless a clinician says otherwise.

No food is universally safe or unsafe. Frequency, portion, and overall pattern decide the educational message.
