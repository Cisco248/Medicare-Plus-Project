# Types of Diabetes

## Question: What are the main types of diabetes?

### Answer

The main types discussed in public-health education are type 1 diabetes, type 2 diabetes, gestational diabetes, and other less common forms. Prediabetes is a related glucose state, not a separate lifelong diabetes type.

### Important safety information

Only a qualified clinician can determine diabetes type. Symptoms and one glucose value cannot reliably separate types.

## Question: What is type 1 diabetes?

### Answer

Type 1 diabetes occurs when the pancreas makes little or no insulin, usually because the immune system has damaged insulin-producing cells.

### Explanation

It can appear in childhood or adulthood. It is not caused by eating sugar and is not caused by low step counts. People with type 1 diabetes need insulin as directed by their care team. The MediCare Plus diabetes model does not classify type 1 versus type 2.

## Question: What is type 2 diabetes?

### Answer

Type 2 diabetes occurs when the body cannot use insulin effectively (insulin resistance) and often cannot make enough insulin to keep glucose in a healthy range.

### Explanation

It is the most common form in adults. Risk is associated with age, family history, overweight or obesity, inactivity, previous gestational diabetes, and other cardiometabolic factors. It can often be present for years with few symptoms. Lifestyle, monitoring, and medicines (when prescribed) are commonly used together. Activity and diet do not replace prescribed care.

## Question: What is gestational diabetes?

### Answer

Gestational diabetes is high blood glucose first recognized during pregnancy.

### Explanation

It needs pregnancy-specific care because it can affect parent and baby. Glucose often returns toward typical ranges after birth, but the person has higher later risk of type 2 diabetes and should discuss postpartum follow-up. The heart-disease e-doc form includes a diabetes option “Yes (during pregnancy)” because pregnancy-related diabetes is a recognized history item in some screening surveys. That checkbox is not a pregnancy diagnosis.

## Question: What is prediabetes?

### Answer

Prediabetes means glucose or HbA1c is higher than typical reference screening ranges but does not meet diabetes diagnostic criteria.

### Explanation

ADA educational criteria include HbA1c 5.7% to 6.4%, fasting plasma glucose 100 to 125 mg/dL (5.6 to 6.9 mmol/L), or a 2-hour oral glucose tolerance result of 140 to 199 mg/dL (7.8 to 11.0 mmol/L). WHO also describes impaired fasting glucose and impaired glucose tolerance with related laboratory ranges. These are screening and diagnostic laboratory concepts, not one home meter reading. Prediabetes increases the chance of later type 2 diabetes and cardiovascular risk; it is not a diagnosis of current diabetes. The hypertension e-doc form uses the category `pre-diabetic` as a diabetes-status input, which is a model category, not a laboratory report.

## Other forms

Less common diabetes includes monogenic diabetes and diabetes related to pancreatic disease or some medicines. These require specialist assessment. The app does not identify these forms.
