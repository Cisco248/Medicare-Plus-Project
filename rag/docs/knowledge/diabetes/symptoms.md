# Diabetes Symptoms

Also asked as: what are diabetes symptoms, how do I feel if I have diabetes, signs of high blood sugar.

## Question: What are the symptoms of diabetes?

### Answer

Commonly described symptoms include increased thirst, urinating more often, increased hunger, unexplained weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing sores, and recurrent infections. Numbness or tingling may occur with nerve involvement but is not specific to diabetes. Many people, especially early in type 2 diabetes, have no obvious symptoms.

### Explanation

Symptoms are experiences, not diagnoses. The same symptoms can have many other causes. CDC and NHS list these features as reasons to seek testing, not as proof of diabetes.

### Important safety information

Sudden severe illness, confusion, loss of consciousness, severe breathing difficulty, or persistent vomiting requires urgent assessment. See diabetes warning signs and emergencies.

## Possible symptoms

- Thirst and dry mouth
- Passing urine more often, including at night
- Hunger
- Tiredness
- Blurred vision
- Weight loss without trying (more classic in type 1, can occur in type 2)
- Slow healing and infections
- Numbness or tingling in hands or feet

## Activity and wearable context

Fatigue or illness can coincide with fewer steps, altered sleep, or a different heart-rate pattern. A wearable cannot determine why those changes occurred. Normal activity does not rule out diabetes, and reduced activity does not confirm it.

## Safe interpretation

- Describe the symptom, duration, severity, and change without assigning a cause.
- Do not treat absent symptoms as evidence of normal glucose.
- Diagnosis requires clinical assessment and appropriate blood testing.
- The diabetes model does not use a symptom checklist. It uses numeric and yes/no parameters such as glucose, BMI, and blood pressure.

## Routine clinical advice

Arrange routine clinical advice when possible symptoms are persistent, recurring, or concerning, particularly when risk factors are also present.
