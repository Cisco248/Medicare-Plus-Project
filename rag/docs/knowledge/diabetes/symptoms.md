# Diabetes Symptoms

## Definition and purpose

Symptoms are experiences reported by a person; they are not diagnoses. Diabetes may cause no obvious symptoms, especially early in type 2 diabetes, and the same symptoms can have many other causes.

## Possible symptoms

Commonly described symptoms include increased thirst, urinating more often, increased hunger, unexplained weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing sores, and recurrent infections. Numbness or tingling may occur with nerve involvement but is not specific to diabetes.

## Activity and wearable context

Fatigue or illness can coincide with fewer steps, altered sleep, or a different heart-rate pattern. A wearable cannot determine why those changes occurred. Normal activity does not rule out diabetes, and reduced activity does not confirm it. Symptom reports and device trends should remain separate evidence.

## Safe interpretation

- Describe the symptom, duration, severity, and change without assigning a cause.
- Do not treat absent symptoms as evidence of normal glucose.
- Do not infer thirst, urination, or weight change from steps, calories, or sleep.
- Diagnosis requires clinical assessment and appropriate blood testing.
- Missing symptom data mean not reported, not absent.

## Routine clinical advice

Arrange routine clinical advice when possible symptoms are persistent, recurring, or concerning, particularly when risk factors are also present. Sudden severe illness, confusion, loss of consciousness, severe breathing difficulty, or persistent vomiting requires urgent assessment as described in `warning_signs.md`.
