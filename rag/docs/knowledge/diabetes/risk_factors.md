# Diabetes Risk Factors

## Definition and purpose

A risk factor is a characteristic associated with a greater chance of disease; it is not proof that disease is present. Risk patterns differ by diabetes type. This page focuses mainly on type 2 diabetes because lifestyle and metabolic factors are most relevant there.

## Key factors

Recognized type 2 diabetes risk factors include family history, increasing age, overweight or obesity, physical inactivity, previous gestational diabetes, prediabetes, and some cardiovascular risk conditions. Risk is shaped by access to healthy food, safe activity, preventive care, and other social circumstances. A person may develop type 2 diabetes without an obvious modifiable risk factor, while another person with several factors may not have diabetes.

## Activity and wearable context

Repeatedly low activity may be one modifiable risk factor, but step counts have no diagnostic cutoff. Device non-wear, mobility limitations, occupation, illness, weather, and sensor error can alter records. Calories are model estimates, not metabolic tests. Interpret trends against a stated personal baseline and alongside clinical history.

## Safe interpretation

Do not combine low steps, short sleep, weight, or heart rate into a fabricated “diabetes diagnosis.” Label each as a measured value, estimated behavior, reported fact, risk factor, or missing datum. Diabetes diagnosis depends on validated clinical testing and interpretation. Do not recommend medication changes.

## Routine clinical advice

Routine advice is appropriate when someone is concerned about multiple risk factors, has a history of gestational diabetes or prediabetes, or wants to know whether clinical screening is appropriate. Screening decisions should be individualized by a qualified professional.
