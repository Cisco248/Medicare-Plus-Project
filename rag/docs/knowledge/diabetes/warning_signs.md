# Diabetes Warning Signs

## Purpose

Most diabetes questions are suitable for routine care, but severe low or high glucose states and acute illness can require urgent help. Symptoms and severity should guide escalation; wearable activity data must not delay it.

## Urgent warning signs

Contact local emergency services for loss of consciousness, seizure, severe confusion, inability to swallow safely, severe breathing difficulty, or rapidly worsening severe illness. A person who is unconscious should not be given food or drink.

Urgent medical assessment is also appropriate for persistent vomiting, marked dehydration, severe weakness, or symptoms suggesting a serious glucose emergency, particularly in someone with known diabetes. Follow an existing clinician-provided emergency or sick-day plan where one is available.

## Safe interpretation

Sweating, shaking, hunger, dizziness, palpitations, or confusion can occur with low glucose but are not specific. Thirst, frequent urination, fatigue, or blurred vision may occur with high glucose and can also have other causes. A device step count, sleep score, calorie estimate, or wrist heart rate cannot determine glucose or diagnose diabetic ketoacidosis.

Do not apply universal glucose thresholds without the person’s care context, device units, and established plan. Do not recommend medicine changes. Do not overstate mild, isolated, non-progressive symptoms as emergencies; recommend routine advice when they persist or recur.

## Routine clinical advice

Discuss recurrent possible low-glucose symptoms, repeated unexpected readings, persistent high-glucose symptoms, or uncertainty about sick-day planning with the clinical team.
