# Diabetes Complications

## Question: What complications can diabetes cause?

### Answer

Persistently elevated blood glucose in diabetes may increase the risk of long-term damage to blood vessels and nerves. Educational complication groups include heart and blood-vessel disease, kidney disease, eye disease, nerve damage, foot problems, skin and dental issues, and acute metabolic emergencies.

### Important safety information

Having diabetes does not mean every complication will occur. A model prediction cannot stage complications. New chest pain, sudden weakness, vision loss, or a foot ulcer needs prompt clinical care.

## Cardiovascular complications

Diabetes is associated with higher risk of coronary heart disease, heart attack, stroke, and peripheral artery disease. Blood pressure, lipids, tobacco, and kidney disease add to that risk. This is why e-doc forms collect blood pressure, cholesterol (hypertension model), and diabetes status (heart-disease model) as related features.

## Kidney, eye, and nerve complications

High glucose and high blood pressure together can injure kidney filters. Diabetic eye disease can impair vision and may have few early symptoms, which is why eye screening is used. Nerve damage can cause numbness, pain, or digestive and sexual symptoms. Numbness in the feet increases injury risk.

## Foot, skin, and dental problems

Reduced sensation, poor circulation, and infections can lead to ulcers. Skin and gum infections can be more frequent. Daily foot checks are educational self-care, not a substitute for clinic review of wounds.

## Acute metabolic complications

Severe hypoglycaemia and severe hyperglycaemia, including diabetic ketoacidosis and hyperosmolar states, are emergencies. See diabetes emergencies knowledge. Wearable step counts cannot diagnose these states.

## Prevention concepts

Glucose, blood pressure, and lipid care, not smoking, activity, foot care, and recommended screening reduce—but do not eliminate—risk. Individual plans belong with the clinical team.
