# Causes of Diabetes

## Question: What causes diabetes?

### Answer

Different types have different mechanisms. Type 1 diabetes is mainly an autoimmune loss of insulin production. Type 2 diabetes involves insulin resistance and a relative insulin shortage, with genetic, metabolic, and environmental contributions. Gestational diabetes reflects pregnancy-related insulin resistance that the pancreas cannot fully overcome.

### Important safety information

Cause is not the same as blame. Many factors are outside a person’s control. The chatbot cannot determine a person’s diabetes type or cause from e-doc inputs.

## Question: What is insulin resistance?

### Answer

Insulin resistance means body tissues respond less effectively to insulin, so more insulin is needed to move glucose into cells.

### Explanation

It is associated with central adiposity, physical inactivity, some sleep disorders, certain medicines, and genetic factors. It is a mechanism, not a diagnosis by itself. High BMI can be associated with insulin resistance but does not prove it. Laboratory insulin levels are not collected by the MediCare Plus diabetes e-doc form.

## Question: What is insulin deficiency?

### Answer

Insulin deficiency means the pancreas does not release enough insulin. In type 1 diabetes this is usually severe. In type 2 diabetes insulin production may decline over time.

## Genetic and family factors

Family history of diabetes is associated with higher type 2 diabetes risk. Shared genes, shared environments, and shared eating or activity patterns can all contribute. The diabetes model uses a yes/no family-diabetes feature (`family_diabetes`). A “Yes” value increases the information the model has about familial risk; it does not mean the user has diabetes.

## Environmental and lifestyle-related factors

Physical inactivity, dietary patterns high in free sugars and ultra-processed foods, tobacco use, and excess body weight are associated with type 2 diabetes risk. They do not cause type 1 diabetes. Social conditions such as food access and safe places to be active also matter.

## Pregnancy-related factors

Previous gestational diabetes and giving birth to a large baby are recognized type 2 diabetes risk factors. They require clinical follow-up rather than self-diagnosis.

## Other clinical factors

Hypertension, cardiovascular disease, polycystic ovary syndrome, and some endocrine or pancreatic conditions can cluster with glucose disorders. Clustering is not proof of cause.

## Model usage

The diabetes prediction model does not output a cause. It uses age, gender, pulse rate, systolic and diastolic blood pressure, glucose in mmol/L, BMI, family history, and hypertensive status as features. Feature importance for one person is not displayed by the app.
