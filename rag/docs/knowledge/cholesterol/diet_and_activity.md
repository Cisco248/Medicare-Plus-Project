# High Cholesterol: Diet and Physical Activity

## Heart-healthy eating pattern

A heart-healthy eating pattern can emphasize:

- Vegetables
- Fruits
- Whole grains
- Beans and legumes
- Nuts
- Fish and other appropriate lean protein sources
- High-fiber foods

It can reduce excessive intake of saturated and trans fats.

## Saturated fat

High saturated-fat intake can raise LDL cholesterol. Food choices should be considered as part of the overall dietary pattern rather than focusing on one food.

## Physical activity

Regular physical activity can help improve cardiovascular fitness and lipid levels. The American Heart Association notes that activity can help lower LDL and raise HDL.

A practical goal for many adults is around 150 minutes of moderate-intensity activity per week, adjusted for fitness and health status.

## Useful activities

- Walking
- Cycling
- Swimming
- Dancing
- Gardening
- Recreational sports
- Strength training

Consistency is generally more useful than choosing an activity that cannot be maintained.

## Weight management

For people with overweight or obesity, appropriate weight management can improve several cardiovascular risk factors. Individual targets should be set with healthcare professionals.

## Smoking

Smoking damages cardiovascular health. Quitting is an important risk-reduction behavior.

## RAG activity relationships

`Regular physical activity -> improved cardiovascular fitness`

`Regular physical activity -> can improve LDL/HDL profile`

`High saturated-fat intake -> may increase LDL`

`Physical inactivity + other risk factors -> greater overall cardiovascular risk`

The RAG system should not claim that a single exercise session will normalize cholesterol.
