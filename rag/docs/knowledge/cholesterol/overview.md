# Cholesterol Overview

## Definition and purpose

Cholesterol is a waxy substance carried in blood within lipoproteins. It is needed for normal body functions, but an unfavorable lipid profile can contribute to atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease. Assessment commonly considers LDL cholesterol, HDL cholesterol, triglycerides, total cholesterol, and the person’s overall cardiovascular risk.

## Key facts

High cholesterol usually has no symptoms. LDL is often described as atherogenic because higher exposure contributes to plaque formation. HDL has a different transport role, but one HDL value should not be interpreted in isolation. Triglycerides are a separate blood fat influenced by multiple factors.

## Activity and wearable context

Regular physical activity supports cardiovascular health and can influence lipid patterns over time. Steps, sleep, heart rate, and calories do not measure cholesterol. A very active person can have an unfavorable lipid profile, and low activity does not establish one.

## Safe interpretation

- Diagnosis and treatment decisions require a lipid test plus clinical context.
- Preserve analyte, value, unit, date, fasting status when relevant, and laboratory source.
- Do not use one result to infer a long-term trend.
- Distinguish a lipid measurement from total cardiovascular risk.
- Missing lipid data are unknown, not normal.
- Do not recommend starting, stopping, or changing lipid-lowering medicine.

## Routine clinical advice

Seek routine advice about whether testing is appropriate, interpretation of results, family history of early cardiovascular disease, or individualized risk reduction. Severe chest or stroke symptoms are emergencies even if cholesterol status is unknown.
