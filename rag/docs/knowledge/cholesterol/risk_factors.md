# Cholesterol Risk Factors

## Definition and purpose

Risk factors can increase the likelihood of an unfavorable lipid profile or increase the cardiovascular importance of a lipid result. A risk factor is not a diagnosis, and cholesterol itself is only one part of cardiovascular risk.

## Key factors

Factors associated with unhealthy cholesterol or triglyceride patterns include genetic conditions and family history, dietary patterns high in saturated or trans fat, insufficient activity, tobacco exposure, obesity, diabetes, some kidney or thyroid conditions, increasing age, and some medicines. Social circumstances can affect food access, activity, testing, and care.

Inherited lipid disorders may produce high risk despite healthy behavior. Conversely, lifestyle risk factors do not prove that laboratory values are abnormal.

## Activity and wearable context

Long-term activity can support cardiovascular health, but no step, heart-rate, sleep, or calorie threshold identifies high LDL. Device estimates should not be combined into an invented cholesterol score. Account for non-wear, disability, illness, and activities that steps do not capture.

## Safe interpretation

Separate modifiable and non-modifiable factors, observed behavior, laboratory evidence, and missing data. Avoid claiming a single food or inactive day caused a result. Overall cardiovascular assessment may include age, blood pressure, diabetes, tobacco exposure, family history, and other clinical factors, but formal risk estimation must use an appropriate validated method. Do not recommend medicine changes.

## Routine clinical advice

Routine advice is appropriate for concerns about family history, whether lipid testing is needed, or how risk factors and results fit together.
