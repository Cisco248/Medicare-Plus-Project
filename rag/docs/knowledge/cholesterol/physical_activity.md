# Cholesterol and Physical Activity

## Purpose and relationship

Regular physical activity supports cardiovascular fitness and can contribute to a healthier lipid profile and overall risk reduction. Activity is one part of prevention; it does not replace lipid testing or prove that arteries are healthy.

## Guidance and activity data

WHO recommends adults complete 150–300 minutes of moderate-intensity aerobic activity, 75–150 minutes of vigorous activity, or an equivalent combination weekly, with muscle strengthening on at least two days. These are population goals and should be adapted when health status, disability, pregnancy, symptoms, or fitness requires it.

Wearables may estimate steps, active minutes, distance, intensity, heart rate, sedentary time, and calories. Step counts omit many activities and calorie estimates are uncertain. No wearable target corresponds directly to LDL or triglyceride values.

## Safe interpretation

Use longitudinal patterns and a stated comparison period. One active week does not normalize cholesterol, and one inactive week does not diagnose it. Correlated changes in activity and lipids do not establish causation because diet, illness, weight, genetics, medicines, and measurement variation may contribute. Missing device data are not sedentary behavior.

Do not prescribe a specific exercise plan or recommend medicine changes. High-intensity activity may require individualized advice when cardiovascular disease or significant symptoms are present.

## Routine clinical advice

Seek routine advice before major changes when there is known cardiovascular disease, substantial inactivity, mobility limitation, or concern about safe exercise. Severe chest pressure, major breathlessness, fainting, or sudden neurological symptoms during activity requires emergency help.
