# Cholesterol Monitoring

## Purpose

Lipid monitoring measures blood fats and supports cardiovascular risk assessment and follow-up. Testing frequency and targets depend on age, health conditions, family history, prior results, and the clinical plan.

## Measurements

A lipid profile may report total cholesterol, LDL cholesterol, HDL cholesterol, and triglycerides. Preserve each analyte, value, unit, date, laboratory, and fasting status when relevant. Units differ between health systems, so values must never be compared without units or silently converted.

A single result is a measurement, not a longitudinal trend. Laboratory and biological variation, recent illness, and testing context can affect interpretation. LDL may be calculated or measured directly; this distinction can matter in some settings.

## Activity and wearable context

Steps, active minutes, sleep, heart rate, and calorie estimates can describe lifestyle context but cannot estimate lipid values or confirm medicine response. Changes that occur in the same period may be associated without proving causation.

## Safe interpretation

- Interpret the full lipid profile with overall cardiovascular risk, not one value in isolation.
- Distinguish an abnormal result, documented diagnosis, risk factor, and trend.
- Missing analytes are unknown, not normal.
- Do not infer adherence or recommend starting, stopping, or changing medicine.
- Do not use consumer wearables as substitutes for laboratory testing.

## Routine clinical advice

Seek routine clinical advice for result interpretation, testing frequency, family history, substantial changes between tests, or individualized risk and targets.
