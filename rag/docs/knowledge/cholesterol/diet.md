# Cholesterol and Diet

## Purpose and relationship

Dietary patterns can affect LDL cholesterol, triglycerides, and overall cardiovascular health. They should be interpreted over time and in the context of genetics, health conditions, access, and preferences.

## Key facts

A heart-healthy pattern emphasizes vegetables, fruits, whole grains, legumes, nuts, fish, and other appropriate lean protein sources. Replacing foods high in saturated and trans fats with unsaturated-fat sources can support a healthier lipid profile. Overall pattern matters more than labeling one food as a cure or cause.

High cholesterol is not simply “eating cholesterol,” and some people have strong inherited risk. Triglycerides and LDL are different measurements and should not be conflated.

## Data and safe interpretation

Food logs are often incomplete and do not establish nutrient absorption or blood lipid levels. Wearable calories burned cannot determine what someone should eat, whether a diet is heart healthy, or whether LDL changed. A single meal is not a long-term dietary pattern. Missing dietary data mean unknown.

Do not prescribe restrictive diets, supplements, or individualized weight targets. Pregnancy, kidney disease, food insecurity, allergies, frailty, and eating disorders require appropriate professional context. Never recommend starting, stopping, or changing medicine.

## Routine clinical advice

Seek routine advice from a qualified clinician or dietitian for individualized dietary support, lipid-result interpretation, inherited risk, or difficulty meeting nutritional needs.
