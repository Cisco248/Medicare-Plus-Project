# Resting and Activity Heart Rate

## Real-world use case

A daily summary shows an average or resting heart rate from a watch, plus higher values during a walk or workout. People ask whether the number is “too high” or “too low.” A consumer reading can add context; it is not an electrocardiogram and does not diagnose arrhythmia.

## Public-health ranges

The American Heart Association describes a usual adult resting heart rate as about 60 to 100 beats per minute when a person is sitting or lying, calm, and feeling well. Resting values below 60 can occur in athletes, during sleep, or with some medicines such as beta blockers, and are not automatically a problem. Resting values over 100 can occur with fever, dehydration, anxiety, pain, caffeine, or illness, and also are not automatically a diagnosis of tachycardia in the clinical sense.

AHA target-heart-rate education uses age-based estimates for exercise intensity. Those formulas are ballpark figures. Individual maximum heart rate varies, and medicines that slow the heart change the expected exercise response.

## Wearable accuracy

Harvard Health has reported that wrist optical heart-rate monitors can be reasonably close at rest for a range of skin tones, but accuracy can shift with activity. Walking has been observed to push some wrist readings high; typing and other wrist motion can push them low. Chest-strap electrical sensors are generally closer to clinical heart-rate measurement when a precise training number is needed.

## Safe interpretation

- Label the value as resting, average, or in-activity, and as device-estimated unless a clinical ECG or pulse check is documented.
- One unusual reading is not a persistent condition. Repeated unexpected resting rates, new palpitations, chest symptoms, fainting, or exercise intolerance need clinical assessment.
- Do not diagnose atrial fibrillation, heart block, or fitness solely from a daily average.
- Severe chest pressure, fainting, or sudden neurological symptoms require emergency care, not another heart-rate sample.
