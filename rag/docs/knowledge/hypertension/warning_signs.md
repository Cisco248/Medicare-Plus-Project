# Hypertension Warning Signs

## Purpose

Hypertension is usually symptomless. Emergency escalation should be based on severe symptoms and appropriately measured blood pressure, not on a wearable trend or the assumption that any headache is a crisis.

## Emergency warning signs

Contact local emergency services for severe chest pain or pressure, major breathing difficulty, collapse or loss of consciousness, or sudden stroke signs such as facial weakness, arm weakness, speech difficulty, severe confusion, or sudden major vision change. Rapidly worsening severe symptoms also need urgent assessment.

If a blood-pressure reading is extremely unusual for the person and severe symptoms are present, do not wait for more activity or sleep data and do not repeatedly measure instead of seeking help. Follow any clinician-provided emergency plan.

## Safe interpretation

Headache, dizziness, nosebleed, anxiety, and palpitations can have many causes and do not prove dangerous hypertension. A single high reading without severe symptoms still deserves calm remeasurement according to validated technique and appropriate clinical advice, but should not automatically be described as an emergency. Exact escalation thresholds can vary by guideline and individual context.

Steps, heart rate, calories, and sleep cannot confirm or exclude a hypertensive emergency. Do not diagnose stroke or heart attack and do not recommend taking extra, stopping, or changing medicine.

## Routine clinical advice

Seek routine or prompt clinical advice for repeated high readings, recurring non-severe symptoms, uncertainty about technique, or readings outside an agreed care plan.
