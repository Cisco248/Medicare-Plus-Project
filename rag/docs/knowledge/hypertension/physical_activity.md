# Hypertension and Physical Activity

## Purpose and relationship

Regular physical activity can help prevent and manage high blood pressure and supports cardiovascular fitness. Benefits develop through sustained patterns; one exercise session does not prove long-term blood-pressure control.

## Guidance and wearable data

WHO recommends adults complete 150–300 minutes of moderate-intensity aerobic activity, 75–150 minutes of vigorous activity, or an equivalent combination weekly, plus muscle strengthening on at least two days. People should also limit sedentary time. These population targets are not prescriptions for every health condition.

Wearables can estimate steps, active minutes, intensity, distance, and heart rate. They do not measure blood pressure unless they are specifically validated for that purpose, and even device-derived pressure data require correct use and clinical context. Step totals miss many activities.

## Safe interpretation

Compare repeated activity with a defined baseline and account for non-wear, disability, illness, and activity type. Reduced activity is a trend, not evidence that pressure rose. Exercise heart-rate response cannot establish blood pressure. Do not recommend abrupt high-intensity exercise or medicine changes.

## Routine clinical advice

People with known hypertension, cardiovascular disease, significant symptoms, mobility limitations, or long inactivity should seek individualized advice when uncertain about safe intensity. Discuss symptoms during activity or repeated pressure concerns with a clinician. Stop activity and seek urgent help for severe chest pressure, major breathing difficulty, fainting, or sudden neurological symptoms.
