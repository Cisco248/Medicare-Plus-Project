# Hypertension Risk Factors

## Definition and purpose

A hypertension risk factor is associated with a greater likelihood of developing high blood pressure or cardiovascular complications. It does not establish current blood pressure or a diagnosis.

## Key factors

Risk can be influenced by increasing age, family history, high-sodium dietary patterns, insufficient physical activity, overweight or obesity, harmful alcohol use, tobacco exposure, diabetes, kidney disease, and social or environmental conditions. Some factors are modifiable and others are not. Risk factors may cluster, but their presence does not reveal an individual’s measured pressure.

## Activity and wearable context

Long-term activity and sedentary-time patterns can provide behavior context. A low step count may reflect disability, acute illness, device non-wear, work pattern, or limited access to safe activity. Wearable heart rate is not a proxy for blood pressure, and calorie estimates do not quantify hypertension risk.

## Safe interpretation

Label each factor explicitly and avoid adding them into an unsupported risk score. Distinguish an association from a cause and a risk factor from a symptom. Diagnosis requires validated blood-pressure assessment, often including repeated clinic, home, or ambulatory readings. Missing blood-pressure data cannot be filled from lifestyle data. Never suggest medication changes.

## Routine clinical advice

Routine clinical advice is appropriate for people concerned about multiple risk factors, family history, kidney or cardiovascular disease, or whether blood-pressure checks are needed. Individual prevention plans should account for health status, access, and preferences.
