# Hypertension Symptoms

## Definition and purpose

Hypertension usually causes no noticeable symptoms. Symptoms cannot reliably identify blood pressure, and feeling well does not prove that pressure is normal.

## Symptoms and associated concerns

Headache, dizziness, blurred vision, nosebleed, fatigue, or palpitations may prompt a blood-pressure check, but each has many possible causes. They should not be labeled “hypertension symptoms” without assessment. Chest discomfort, severe breathing difficulty, fainting, or sudden neurological symptoms require urgent evaluation because they may indicate a serious condition, not because they prove hypertension.

## Wearable context

A wearable may show a heart-rate or activity change during discomfort, stress, illness, or exercise. It cannot measure the cause and generally cannot substitute for a validated blood-pressure device. Normal steps or heart rate do not rule out hypertension; low steps or poor sleep do not confirm it.

## Safe interpretation

Record what was reported, onset, duration, severity, associated features, and available validated pressure readings. Keep symptom reports separate from measurements. Do not infer blood pressure from a symptom, and do not infer symptoms from device data. Missing symptom data mean not reported. Do not recommend medicine changes.

## Routine clinical advice

Seek routine clinical advice for recurring non-severe symptoms, repeated concerning pressure readings, or questions about measurement and risk. Contact local emergency services for severe chest pressure, major breathing difficulty, collapse, or sudden stroke signs.
