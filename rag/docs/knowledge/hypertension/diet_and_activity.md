# Hypertension: Diet, Physical Activity and Lifestyle

## Sodium

High sodium intake can raise blood pressure. WHO recommends adults consume less than 2000 mg sodium per day, equivalent to less than 5 g of salt per day, unless a healthcare professional gives different advice for a particular clinical situation.

Common sodium sources include:

- Processed foods
- Instant foods
- Salty snacks
- Processed meats
- Sauces and condiments
- Foods prepared with large amounts of salt

## Physical activity

Regular activity can reduce blood pressure and improve cardiovascular fitness.

Useful activities include:

- Brisk walking
- Cycling
- Swimming
- Dancing
- Running when appropriate
- Strength training

WHO recommends at least 150 minutes per week of moderate-intensity aerobic activity or 75 minutes of vigorous activity, plus strength-building activity on at least 2 days per week.

## Sedentary behavior

Reducing prolonged sitting and increasing everyday movement can support cardiovascular health.

Examples:

- Walking short distances
- Standing and moving during long sitting periods
- Household activities
- Gardening
- Taking movement breaks

## Weight

If a person is overweight or obese, appropriate weight management can contribute to blood pressure improvement. Targets should be individualized.

## Tobacco and alcohol

Smoking increases cardiovascular risk. Excessive alcohol consumption can contribute to hypertension. Tobacco cessation and reducing harmful alcohol use are important components of cardiovascular risk reduction.

## Stress and sleep

Stress management and adequate sleep can support healthier cardiovascular behavior. Persistent sleep problems should be discussed with a healthcare professional.

## RAG activity relationship

`High sodium intake -> higher blood pressure risk`

`Physical inactivity -> higher hypertension risk`

`Regular aerobic activity -> supports lower blood pressure and cardiovascular fitness`

`Prolonged sedentary time -> less favorable cardiovascular behavior`

These are population-level relationships and do not predict an individual's blood pressure from one behavior.
