# Hypertension and Diet

## Purpose and relationship

Dietary patterns influence blood pressure and overall cardiovascular risk. Food guidance should describe population evidence without assuming that a food log explains an individual reading.

## Key facts

WHO recommends adults consume less than 2,000 mg sodium per day, equivalent to less than 5 g salt, while recognizing that individual clinical advice may differ. Much sodium comes from processed foods, breads, processed meats, snacks, sauces, and food prepared with salt. A pattern rich in vegetables, fruits, whole grains, legumes, and appropriate protein sources can support cardiovascular health. Harmful alcohol use can contribute to high blood pressure.

## Data and safe interpretation

Food records are commonly incomplete, and packaging or restaurant estimates can be imprecise. Sodium intake cannot be inferred from steps, calories burned, sleep, or heart rate. One salty meal does not diagnose hypertension, and one lower-sodium day does not establish control. Missing dietary data are unknown.

Do not prescribe an individualized diet, fluid target, or supplement. Kidney disease, pregnancy, frailty, eating disorders, and other conditions may alter appropriate advice. Do not recommend starting, stopping, or changing blood-pressure medicine.

## Routine clinical advice

Seek routine advice from a clinician or qualified dietitian for personalized sodium or eating guidance, repeated blood-pressure concerns, kidney disease, or difficulty maintaining adequate nutrition.
