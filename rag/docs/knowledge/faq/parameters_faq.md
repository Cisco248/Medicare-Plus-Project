# Parameter and Value FAQ

## What is BMI?

Body mass index from height and weight. CDC adult categories: underweight <18.5, healthy 18.5–24.9, overweight 25–29.9, obesity ≥30.

## What does glucose mean?

Blood sugar. Diabetes e-doc unit is mmol/L.

## What does blood pressure mean?

Force of blood in arteries, systolic/diastolic mmHg.

## What does insulin mean?

A pancreas hormone that moves glucose into cells. Not an e-doc numeric feature.

## What does cholesterol mean?

A blood fat carried in lipoproteins. Hypertension e-doc uses mg/dL total-style cholesterol.

## What does HbA1c mean?

Two-to-three-month average glucose exposure, in percent on the hypertension form.

## Why is age important? Why is weight important?

Age is a non-modifiable risk marker. Weight feeds BMI. Neither diagnoses disease.

## Is my glucose high? What does a high glucose value mean? What does a low glucose value mean?

High: interpret unit and timing; laboratory fasting ≥7.0 mmol/L is a diagnostic-range conversation. Low: below about 3.9 mmol/L can be a hypo alert, especially with symptoms.

## Is my BMI high? What does a BMI of 31 mean? What does a BMI of 29.5 mean?

31 is CDC obesity class screening; 29.5 is overweight screening. Not a diagnosis.

## Is my blood pressure high? What does an elevated blood pressure reading mean?

See AHA educational chart. One reading is not persistent hypertension.

## Why is my age included? Why is my blood pressure included? Why is glucose used?

They are real model features for diabetes (age, BP, glucose) as documented in models/diabetes-model.md.

## Which parameter is causing my risk?

The app does not output feature importance. Multiple features are used together.
