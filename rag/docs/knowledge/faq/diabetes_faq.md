# Diabetes FAQ

Use these question headings for retrieval. Answers are educational, not diagnoses.

## What is diabetes?

Diabetes is a group of chronic conditions in which blood glucose stays too high because of insulin deficiency, insulin resistance, or both. Also asked as: explain diabetes, tell me about diabetes, what does diabetes mean, what is diabetes mellitus, what is high blood sugar.

## How can I control diabetes?

Combine clinician follow-up, monitoring as advised, a sustainable eating pattern, activity as appropriate, and prescribed medicines only. Also asked as: how can I control or manage this disease, how do I manage diabetes, how to control blood sugar.

## Which diet plans are suitable?

There is no single correct diet. Emphasize vegetables, fruits, whole grains, pulses, and appropriate protein; limit sugary drinks and free sugars. Sri Lankan foods such as rice, red rice, hoppers, roti, dhal, fish, and sambols can fit with portion awareness. Also asked as: what should I eat, diabetes meal plan, which diet plans are suitables.

## How is diabetes identified?

Laboratory fasting glucose, OGTT, HbA1c, or random glucose with classic symptoms, interpreted by a clinician. Symptoms help but are not enough. Also asked as: how the identify the diabetes, how do I know if I have diabetes, how is diabetes diagnosed.

## What medications are used for diabetes?

Insulin is required in type 1 diabetes. Type 2 diabetes may use metformin and other classes, or insulin, when prescribed. Do not start or stop medicines from this chat.

## What does glucose mean?

Glucose is blood sugar. The diabetes e-doc uses mmol/L. See parameters/glucose.md.

## Is my glucose high?

It depends on unit and timing. Fasting laboratory values ≥ 7.0 mmol/L enter the ADA diabetes diagnostic conversation. Below 3.9 mmol/L can be a hypoglycaemia alert for people who monitor. A value of 180 is often mg/dL, not mmol/L.

## What does my glucose value mean?

Use unit, timing, and symptoms. One e-doc number is not a diagnosis.

## Why is glucose used in my diabetes prediction?

Because `glucose` is an actual scaled feature of the diabetes model.

## What does BMI mean? Is my BMI high?

BMI is kg/m². CDC adult overweight starts at 25 and obesity at 30. BMI does not diagnose diabetes.

## Why does the diabetes model use BMI?

`bmi` is a trained feature associated with insulin resistance and type 2 diabetes risk.

## Why is age used by the diabetes model?

Age is a type 2 diabetes risk factor and a scaled feature named `age`.

## What does blood pressure have to do with diabetes?

They cluster and jointly raise vascular risk. The model uses systolic, diastolic, and a hypertensive flag.

## What does insulin mean?

Insulin is the hormone that helps glucose enter cells. The diabetes form does not collect insulin dose.

## What does a diabetes risk prediction mean?

Class 1 is “Diabetic / High Risk”; class 0 is “Non-Diabetic / Low Risk.” Not a diagnosis.

## Does a positive prediction mean I have diabetes?

No.

## Can one abnormal parameter mean I have diabetes?

No.

## Can a normal parameter guarantee that I do not have diabetes?

No.

## Why did the e-doc ask me for these parameters?

They are the diabetes model’s input features.

## Which parameter is most important?

The app does not expose feature importance.

## What should I do during a diabetes emergency?

Call emergency services for unconsciousness, seizure, severe confusion, or inability to swallow. Do not give food or drink to an unconscious person.

## What causes diabetes?

Type 1: autoimmune insulin deficiency. Type 2: insulin resistance and relative insulin deficiency with genetic and environmental factors.

## Can I exercise with diabetes?

Usually yes if a clinician agrees. WHO adult activity ranges are population guidelines. People on insulin need hypo-prevention advice.
