# Estimated Calories and Daily Energy Use

## Real-world use case

A fitness app shows “calories burned” after a walk, a desk day, or a mixed workout. Users often treat that number as a precise energy budget they can eat back. Consumer calorie totals are model estimates, not laboratory measurements of energy expenditure.

## What the number usually is

Wearable and phone calories typically combine a resting-energy estimate from age, sex, height, and weight with an activity add-on from movement or heart rate. Errors grow when profile data are missing, the device is not worn, or the activity is poorly captured (cycling, strength work, load carrying). Active-energy and total-energy fields are not interchangeable.

AHA diet and lifestyle pages emphasize overall eating patterns, sodium awareness, and regular activity rather than matching a single device calorie target. WHO healthy-diet guidance likewise focuses on food quality and dietary patterns.

## Safe interpretation

- Report estimated calories only when they were recorded, with the device source if known.
- Do not infer diet quality, fasting, overeating, or weight change from one day’s calorie estimate.
- Do not invent a calorie deficit or surplus.
- Weight change, when recorded over weeks, is a more direct trend than a daily calorie score, and still has many non-diet causes (hydration, illness, menstrual cycle, medicines).
- Never recommend a specific calorie diet, supplement, or medicine change.

## When to suggest routine advice

Persistent unintended weight change, restrictive eating concerns, or uncertainty about nutrition with diabetes, kidney disease, pregnancy, or frailty belong with a clinician or registered dietitian, not with a wearable score.
