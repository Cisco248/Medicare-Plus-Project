# Exercise Guidelines

## Purpose

Population guidelines describe activity associated with broad health benefits. They are not individualized exercise prescriptions or diagnostic standards.

## Adult guidance

WHO recommends that adults:

- complete 150–300 minutes of moderate-intensity aerobic activity each week; or
- complete 75–150 minutes of vigorous-intensity aerobic activity; or
- use an equivalent combination; and
- perform muscle-strengthening activity involving major muscle groups on at least two days each week.

Adults should limit sedentary time, and replacing it with physical activity of any intensity can be beneficial. Some activity is better than none, and activity can be accumulated across the week.

## Data and safe interpretation

Moderate intensity generally means noticeably raised breathing and heart rate while still allowing conversation; individual effort varies. Wearable “active minutes” may use proprietary definitions and should not automatically be equated with guideline minutes. Step counts are not part of the cited minute-based guideline and have no universal disease-diagnostic threshold.

Record activity type, duration, intensity method, and completeness. Do not conclude that meeting a target rules out disease or that missing it proves disease. Calories and heart rate alone do not determine whether exercise is safe. Do not recommend medicine changes.

## Routine clinical advice

People who are pregnant, frail, acutely ill, substantially inactive, or living with cardiovascular disease, diabetes complications, mobility limitations, or exercise symptoms may need individualized advice. Stop and seek urgent help for severe chest pressure, major breathing difficulty, fainting, or sudden neurological symptoms.
