# Sedentary Behavior

## Definition and purpose

Sedentary behavior is waking behavior with very low energy expenditure while sitting, reclining, or lying. It is different from physical inactivity: someone may meet exercise guidelines and still spend long periods sedentary.

## Key facts

Higher sedentary time is associated with less favorable health outcomes. Public-health guidance encourages limiting sedentary time and replacing it with movement of any intensity when feasible. The appropriate pattern depends on work, mobility, disability, illness, and safety.

## Wearable data

Devices infer sedentary time from movement and posture signals and can misclassify standing still, seated exercise, wheelchair use, device non-wear, or charging. Low steps do not necessarily mean high sedentary time. A “move reminder” is a device prompt, not a clinical finding.

## Safe interpretation

Prefer repeated patterns with wear-time completeness and a stated comparison period. “Recorded sedentary time increased relative to the prior four weeks” is a trend. It is not a diagnosis or proof of cardiovascular or metabolic harm in one individual. Missing data should remain missing, and a partial day should not be compared with a complete day without qualification.

Do not assign disease from sedentary data, impose a universal sitting cutoff, or recommend medicine changes. Recommendations to interrupt sitting should remain general and account for safety and ability.

## Routine clinical advice

Seek routine advice if pain, breathlessness, dizziness, mobility limitations, or illness prevents normal movement, or if a substantial unexplained behavior change persists.
