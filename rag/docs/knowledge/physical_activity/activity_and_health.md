# Physical Activity and Health

## Definition and purpose

Physical activity is bodily movement during leisure, transport, work, household tasks, and daily living. Exercise is planned physical activity, but health-supporting movement is broader than formal exercise.

## Key facts

Regular activity supports cardiovascular and metabolic health, physical function, mental wellbeing, and sleep. Benefits can occur before a person reaches population guideline levels. Activity needs and abilities vary; disability-adapted movement and everyday activity are valid forms.

## Wearable and patient-activity data

Common fields include steps, active minutes, distance, activity type, intensity, sedentary time, heart rate, and estimated calories. These are device-dependent estimates. Steps omit swimming, cycling, resistance work, wheelchair movement, and some occupational activity. Heart rate reflects many influences, and calories are not direct measurements of energy balance.

## Safe interpretation

Use a defined time window, personal baseline, completeness information, and device source. Separate low steps from high sedentary time and both from missing data. One low day may reflect rest, illness, weather, work, disability, or non-wear. Activity does not diagnose diabetes, hypertension, high cholesterol, sleep disorders, or cardiovascular events.

Avoid moral judgments and unsupported universal step targets. Describe trends and uncertainty, and never recommend medicine changes. Symptoms and validated clinical measurements take precedence over activity scores.

## Routine clinical advice

Seek routine advice when symptoms limit activity, activity falls persistently without an obvious explanation, or health conditions make safe intensity uncertain. Severe chest pressure, major breathing difficulty, fainting, or sudden neurological symptoms during activity require emergency help.
